# Editorial checks

Use the checks that fit the assignment. Keep the review internal unless the user asks to see it.

**Intent and truth:** Does the piece say what the author actually means? Did editing add a motive, anecdote, credential, certainty, or concession? Check names, quantities, quotations, and factual qualifications against the supplied evidence.

Read added claims separately from the prose's fluency. Does the source establish each one, or does it merely sound plausible? Keep interpretations properly scoped. An explanation for a missing fact, an unstated mental state, or a research method is still an addition even when it fits the story. A length target does not authorize invention.

**Opening:** Does the first sentence enter the subject, or could it introduce hundreds of unrelated pieces? Does the reader need context before the point, or has context become a delaying ritual?

**Movement:** Read only the first sentences of the paragraphs, then read the entire piece. Does the thought develop? Are several paragraphs doing the same job? Are important ideas rushed to maintain a tidy balance? Preserve necessary structure in technical, instructional, or navigational writing.

**Cadence:** Look at consecutive sentences and paragraphs rather than one sentence in isolation. Do they repeat the same construction? Are long and short sentences motivated by meaning? Does any change of pace clarify emphasis, or merely perform spontaneity?

For each major structural change, name its function. Compare a batch of related drafts for a repeated new house style: spare declaratives, one-line pivots, unexplained jumps, or the same sort of final image. Preserve legitimate shared conventions and the author's supported habits; variation is not an end in itself.

In a single piece, examine successive openings and endings. If different material repeatedly receives the same rhetorical treatment, check whether that treatment belongs to the author and task or has become an automatic fallback. No fixed count, paragraph length, or sentence pattern establishes a problem by itself.

**Specificity:** Do descriptions point to something observable or defensible? Are emotion and personality announced instead of conveyed? Would removing a metaphor make the passage more exact?

**Ending:** Has the work already finished? Cut a summary that only repeats what readers just read. Keep a conclusion when it draws a consequence, resolves the task, or is requested by the form.

**Voice:** Compare with relevant user-authored samples and confirmed preferences. Preserve productive peculiarities. Do not confuse profanity, imperfection, or ornate language with human voice.

Keep the author's effective original passages wherever possible. When no comparable samples exist, assess genre fit and known preferences rather than reporting an established author match. The human-writing research reference offers composing questions, not a replacement author's voice.

When comparing revisions, use the same source material and brief. Ask which version preserves intent and sounds more recognizable, and identify the passage responsible. User feedback is the deciding evidence for personal voice; surface measurements supplement it.
