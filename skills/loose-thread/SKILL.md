# Loose Thread

Write from the author's material, intention, and way of noticing. Let the movement of thought determine the structure. Use the instructions quietly; deliver the requested writing without announcing a style procedure.

## Establish the voice

Use relevant user-authored text and explicit corrections already available before asking for samples. If the active instructions identify a private voice profile, read it. For sustained work, consult [voice-calibration.md](references/voice-calibration.md). Distinguish the user's writing from assistant drafts, quoted opponents, and pasted third-party text. A rejected assistant draft is evidence of a failure, not a positive voice exemplar.

Adapt to the current genre. A frustrated chat message does not establish the voice of the author's book. Preserve intentional bluntness, warmth, humor, anger, or uncertainty when the assignment calls for it. Do not automatically soften the author into polite institutional prose or exaggerate a trait into a persona.

When evidence is sparse, use the known preferences and the conventions of the present task. Do not call that a verified match to the author's voice. Avoid imposing literary minimalism on a letter, or chat cadence on an essay. Ask for missing author material only when it materially changes the assignment and cannot be retrieved within the authorized scope. A short routine draft need not carry an invitation to correct the voice.

When editing user-authored prose, preserve effective sentences, associations, emphasis, and paragraph turns. Make selective changes where possible. A fluent wholesale rewrite can erase the very evidence needed to preserve the author.

## Find the material that matters

Locate the concrete observation, contradiction, question, or experience that gives this piece a reason to exist. Develop its implications before imposing an outline. In an argument, this may be the precise inference that fails; in a bio, a telling fact about a person; in an essay, an observation that opens a larger question.

The published excerpt from Stephen Harrod Buhner's *Ensouling Language* describes attending to a promising thread, rendering experience concretely, and revising words against the experience they should convey. Apply those principles through the author's supplied material. Consult [sources.md](references/sources.md) for attribution and research limits. These instructions are an original adaptation, not a reconstruction of the whole book. Do not pretend to experience the author's feelings or a writing trance.

Apply the congruence test against the emphasis, tone, and meaning present in the author's own words. Ask whether the draft carries those qualities accurately. When the intended feeling is unknown, preserve the evidence and invite clarification only if needed; do not supply an imagined emotional state.

## Build from the author's decisions

For substantial work, read [human-writing-research.md](references/human-writing-research.md) and use its questions about purpose, reader, developing meaning, and author variation. These are ways to make choices, not a prescribed sequence.

Establish who is speaking to whom, what occasions the piece, and what the author wants the reader to notice or understand. Ground the stance in the author's material. Follow an association, complication, example, or inference when it belongs to that material; do not insert a turn merely to break a pattern. Decide what needs emphasis, what the reader can infer, and where more explanation is actually needed.

As a draft develops, reconsider its organization if a supported connection changes what matters. An outline can change; the author's position cannot be silently replaced. Preserve unresolved questions where the evidence leaves them unresolved. Do not stage ignorance, epiphanies, or an emotional journey for effect.

## Let the thought make its shape

- Start where the reader needs to enter. Skip generic scene-setting, praise, and announcements of what the piece will discuss.
- Give each paragraph a reason to exist: make a claim, examine evidence, notice a detail, turn the thought, or let something land. Its role follows from its place in the developing piece, not rotation through a list of functions. Paragraphs may differ in size because their jobs differ.
- Let sentence length and syntax follow emphasis, explanation, hesitation, and momentum. Keep a strong existing cadence. Do not insert random fragments or manufacture numerical variation.
- Allow unequal emphasis. Develop what deserves attention; do not give every point the same space to make the outline look balanced.
- Connect thoughts through their actual relationship. Do not paste a transition onto every paragraph.
- Use headings, bullets, and tables when the task benefits from navigation, instructions, or comparison. Technical writing and correspondence may need explicit organization to make relationships or actions clear.
- Continuous prose does not need to become an outline merely because it is easy to generate one. Choose the form for this reader and task.
- End when the piece has done its work. A decision, consequence, unresolved question, or final detail may finish it. Do not automatically recap or attach a moral.

Preserve useful conventions of the form, including greetings, sign-offs, thesis statements, navigation, instructions, and explicit conclusions when appropriate. Human prose can be elaborate, orderly, lyrical, technical, blunt, or spare. Do not default to short declarative sentences and abrupt endings as a universal human style.

## Keep language attached to something

Choose details that sharpen the meaning. Replace abstractions with an action, example, observation, or consequence when the source supports it. Technical terms can be the most accurate words; explain them where the reader needs help. Avoid decorative metaphors and vague spiritual language unless they belong to the author's chosen register.

When the material does not support a concrete detail, retain the right level of abstraction or identify the missing information. Plausibility is not evidence. A missing supplier figure does not establish that nobody replied; the word "study" does not establish which methods were used. Do not put unsupplied beliefs or future intentions into a nonfiction subject's mind. The author's supplied reflections may be expressed; other interpretations must remain clearly attributed, conditional, or unresolved, and must serve the piece.

Preserve meaningful repetitions, distinctive diction, and productive ambiguity. Remove padding and accidental repetition without sanding every sentence into the same polished register. A clear ordinary sentence may carry more force than a clever slogan.

Do not invent experiences, dialogue, motives, memories, sensory details, credentials, evidence, or certainty. If missing material is essential, ask for it; otherwise write within what is known. For nonfiction, accuracy and the author's actual position constrain every stylistic choice.

## Revise the structure, then the surface

Read the draft as a continuous piece before polishing individual sentences. Look for model habits that displace the author's intention:

- A generic opening followed by evenly sized points and a redundant closing summary.
- Repeated rhetorical contrasts such as "not X, but Y"; staged questions immediately answered; neat three-part lists regardless of the material.
- Uniform paragraph roles, sentence openings, clause shapes, or reassuring tone across an entire piece.
- Praise, hedging, caveats, intensifiers, or grand claims that serve no specific purpose.
- Frequent labels for emotions or personality where an actual observation would convey them.

These are editorial warning signs, not banned devices or proof of AI authorship. Change the passage when the pattern weakens the thought or contradicts the author's preferences. Do not replace one repeated template with another.

For a changed sentence or paragraph, identify what the change does for meaning, emphasis, the reader, or the author's cadence. If its only purpose is to look less machine-written, reconsider it. Across a set of drafts, watch for a repeated fallback: constant terseness, one-line pivots, the same kind of ending, or obligatory unexpected turns. Compare like genres before treating a shared form as a problem.

Then listen to the cadence: where does the sentence need to gather speed, slow down, turn, or stop? Make small word and syntax changes that preserve meaning and the intended feeling. Check that the revision is more precise, not merely more ornamental.

Before delivering, check the opening, paragraph movement, ending, factual fidelity, and fit with the available author samples. For a consequential or extended piece, use [revision-checks.md](references/revision-checks.md). Return the requested prose; explain substantive editorial decisions only when useful or requested.

Check every added factual or psychological claim against its source. Distinguish facts, supported inferences, and interpretation; remove any unnecessary unsupported addition. Source fidelity takes priority over achieving a requested length. If the material cannot sustain that length, say so briefly instead of padding it with invented context.

## Learn from the collaboration

Treat explicit user corrections as strong evidence. Infer preferences cautiously from samples. With authorization to maintain a private profile, record the correction narrowly and keep separate notes for different genres. Do not turn a local request for sarcasm, profanity, brevity, or lyricism into a permanent rule for everything.

The goal is writing the author recognizes and readers can follow. Do not promise to erase a model fingerprint or pass an AI detector. Do not add mistakes, fabricated memories, or artificial randomness to simulate humanity.
